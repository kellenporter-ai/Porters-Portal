The first 3 minutes

Nothing here is graded and nothing here is timed per problem. A week off means cold hands, so the warm-up is the easiest four questions in the set, and they are the same moves you need for the whole 90.

Take a pencil, not a keyboard, for the diagrams. The rule for the whole period: you commit an answer before you check it. Checking first tells you nothing about what you actually know.

Do the sections in order, warm-up, then A, then B, then C. If you are still in A at minute 30, that is information, not failure. Flag it and move.

Minute 35 checkpoint (end of Station A)

Pencils down for thirty seconds and look at what you have on paper. If you left a problem blank because you did not know where to start, mark it with a star. You are coming back to those stars in the last five minutes. Station B is the same idea with strings instead of ramps, so the free-body habit carries straight over.

Nothing is collected at this checkpoint. It is a self-audit, not a hand-in.

Minute 70 checkpoint (end of Station B)

Everything in Station C except C1 and C5 is conceptual. Nothing else there needs a calculator, so if you find yourself reaching for one on another item, stop and reread the question. Two things I want you to leave with: the horizontal part of a projectile's motion does not care about gravity, and gravity points the same way the whole flight. Show me on paper what that means for the top of the arc.

If you finish early

Go back to any problem from A or B where you got the number right but could not have explained the free-body diagram to a classmate. Redraw that diagram on a fresh sheet with no numbers on it, only arrows and labels.

When that is done, check a neighbor's answer against the set and say out loud which specific diagram arrow that neighbor had right or wrong.
